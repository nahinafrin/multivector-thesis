# Reconciliation note — one coherent story across related results

Written to close out limitation #10 (two internal result narratives never merged) and
the §3-vs-Addendum recall discrepancy, both flagged in `multivector-thesis-analysis.md`.
This is a standalone note; `UNIFIED_RESULTS.md` is generated by
`step4 dataset/build_unified_results_report.py`, which now includes the hand-verified
Protocol B context and the current-code seven-layer ablation results. This file adds
the connective narrative and the recall-convention side-by-side.

## 1. The two ASR narratives, reconciled

**Narrative A ("proven negative"), `RESULTS_multivector_methodology.md`:** the conjunctive
multi-vector detector fires validly on 0/34 attack rows — a structural, proven-not-just-
observed failure (Saturation Impossibility). Zero canary leaks / 74/74 poison chunks
redacted regardless, via defense-in-depth (sanitization + canary + grounding).

**Narrative B (ground-plus, original Protocol B), `mitigation_run/` +
`UNIFIED_RESULTS.md`:** after the graded-margin fix, the multivector detector's fire
rate is 24/30 (80%); separately, the ground-plus pipeline's ASR on the planted200 slice
drops 30.5%→16.5% (45.9% relative, McNemar p=2.46e-7, after the marker-matching scorer
bug fix).

**Narrative C (current-code follow-up), `mitigation_results/ablation200/`:** a later
seven-layer implementation was evaluated on the 200-row planted-attack and 200-row
benign-control arms. The full stack measured 3.5% ASR and 8.5% benign blocking. This is
a controlled follow-up within the marker/ASR protocol, not a direct like-for-like
comparison with the earlier six-layer full run or a replacement for the separately
reported ground-plus arm.

**These are not in tension — they measure different things, at different times, and
that's the point of presenting them together:**
- Narrative A is the *pre-fix* baseline result on the *canary-based* protocol (does the
  multivector detector itself fire?). It stays true and citable as the founding negative
  result that motivated the graded-margin fix.
- Narrative B is the *post-fix* result on the marker/ASR protocol, evaluated on
  planted200 rather than the 30/34-row canary slice.
- The correct thesis framing is chronological, not competing: "the detector was proven
  incapable (Narrative A) → the mechanism was fixed and revalidated (0/30→24/30) →
  ground-plus was evaluated under the original marker/ASR protocol (Narrative B) →
  the later seven-layer implementation received a controlled ablation (Narrative C)."
  Present each with its version, arm, and sample size; do not imply that the two
  full-stack estimates came from identical implementations.

## 2. Input-gate recall: report both conventions, don't pick one

| | BLOCK-only (Addenda 3/5 convention) | REVIEW-as-positive (`--review-as-red`) | §3's original figure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Precision | 0.940 | 0.905 | 0.937 |
| Recall | 0.636 | 0.753 | 0.771 |
| F1 | 0.758 | 0.822 | 0.846 |

State plainly in the thesis: §3's figure used a REVIEW-inclusive evaluation convention
(every REVIEW-flagged row counted as caught), while the deployed-gate figures in
Addenda 3/5 use a stricter BLOCK-only convention matching what the gate actually returns
to callers. REVIEW-as-positive (0.753) closes ~85% of the gap to §3's number — a
well-evidenced, mechanistically-explained match, not an exact byte-for-byte
reconciliation (a small residual 0.018 recall gap is unaccounted for and should be
stated as such). Report both conventions side by side in the Results table; do not
present either alone as "the" recall number.

## 4. Current-code seven-layer ablation

The 30 completed runs comprise eight attack leave-one-out configurations, eight benign
leave-one-out configurations, seven attack add-one-in configurations, and seven benign
add-one-in configurations. Each arm has 200 rows, uses the current seven-layer code and
one seed, and reuses the undefended baseline. The baseline measured 61/200 attack
successes (30.5%) and 115/200 benign answers correct (57.5%). The full stack measured
7/200 attack successes (3.5%), 17/200 benign rows blocked (8.5%), and 104/200 benign
answers correct (52.0%).

Removing sanitization produced the largest leave-one-out ASR increase (22/200, 11.0%);
removing grounding, reranking, refuse-on-detection, or the guarded prompt also raised
the observed ASR above the full stack. Removing Presidio yielded 4.0%, while removing
DLP yielded 3.5%. These are descriptive arm differences: the scorer's exact p-values
compare each configuration with undefended, not pairwise with the full stack.

As standalone controls, grounding-only, sanitization-only, and refuse-only measured
15.0%, 16.0%, and 18.0% ASR, respectively. Guarded-prompt-only measured 53.0%, worse
than the 30.5% undefended rate; tightened-rerank-only measured 24.5% (p=0.096 versus
undefended), while Presidio-only and DLP-only each measured 29.5% (p=0.804). Those
standalone comparisons do not establish the contribution of a layer in combination.

The old full-stack result (13.5% ASR with 45.0% benign blocking) is retained as an
older-code cross-check only. It is not the benign-cost estimate for the current
seven-layer stack. Detailed counts are in `step4 dataset/ablation_table.json`; per-run
outputs and manifests are in `step4 dataset/mitigation_results/ablation200/`.
