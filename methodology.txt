================================================================================
MODIFIED METHODOLOGY:
A Zero-Cost, Local, Adaptive RAG Security Extension
================================================================================

This pipeline wraps a Retrieval-Augmented Generation system in a thirteen-stage
defensive cascade. The unifying design principle is an ADAPTIVE RISK SCORE that
flows forward through every stage -- a borderline-suspicious prompt does not
just get logged at the gate; it raises the strictness of context sanitization,
reranking, grounding, and the output rails for that same request. Every
component runs locally on commodity hardware and is open-source or permissively
licensed, so the entire defense costs nothing per query and has no third-party
dependency that could leak prompts off-host.

Two components extend the forward cascade beyond a single pass. A MULTI-VECTOR
RISK FUSION detector (Section 10A) watches the injection-bearing channels --
the query-side gate score and the context-side per-chunk score from Step 6 --
and fires on the CONJUNCTIVE case where both are softly active even though
neither alone crosses its block threshold. A CLOSED-LOOP RISK-FEEDBACK
CONTROLLER (Section 10B) wraps the retrieval-to-grounding segment and, using
the late signals the forward pass already computes (ensemble disagreement,
grounding faithfulness, canary integrity, multi-vector co-activation), decides
to accept, escalate-and-re-verify, broaden-and-retry, or refuse. Both are
local and add no per-query cost on the common path.


================================================================================
0.  DATASETS USED (and which step each one drives)
================================================================================

Three datasets are used at three distinct points in the system. None of them
are paid; all are downloadable from HuggingFace, with the Kaggle-sourced
slices already merged into the local JSONL files committed to the project.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0.1  rag-datasets/rag-mini-wikipedia    (HuggingFace -- knowledge base + clean QA)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two configs of this dataset are used independently:

  (a) text-corpus / passages split
      Used by:   Step 5 (Vector Search)
      How:       download_dataset.py pulls it as JSONL; then
                 kb_rag_mini_wikipedia.py --ingest --index ./kb_wiki encodes
                 every passage with the bge-m3 embedder and writes the FAISS
                 index sidecars (kb_wiki.faiss + kb_wiki.texts).
      Why:       It is a small (3.2k passages), self-contained, factually
                 verifiable Wikipedia subset. Small enough that retrieval
                 timings are deterministic and reproducible; factually
                 verifiable so faithfulness errors are obvious; and free
                 of personal data so the corpus itself does not introduce
                 PII confounds into Step 11 evaluation.

  (b) question-answer / test split
      Used by:   Step 1 input feed (evaluation mode);
                 Step 10 grounding judge (as gold-answer reference);
                 build_adversarial_slice.py (as the benign-control pool).
      How:       Read as JSONL, one record per question with ground-truth
                 answer. Records flow through the full pipeline exactly as a
                 live user prompt would, but with their ground_truth label
                 retained for scoring.
      Why:       Provides labelled benign questions whose correct answers
                 actually live in the (a) corpus. This is what gives us
                 SPECIFICITY evidence -- proof that the cascade does not
                 over-block clean queries.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0.2  Compiled attack dataset           (HuggingFace + Kaggle, merged locally)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Path:         dataset/merged_output/dataset_all.clean.jsonl  (+ train/test/val splits)
Schema:       {"prompt": str, "safety": "safe"|"unsafe", "attack_type": str, ...}
Used by:      Step 3 (Injection Detection -- Input Gate) for evaluation;
              Step 3c (Fusion Gate) calibration and threshold tuning;
              build_adversarial_slice.py (as the adversarial-query pool).
How:          Multiple public prompt-injection / jailbreak / toxicity corpora
              from HuggingFace and Kaggle (e.g. deepset prompt-injection,
              jailbreak-prompts, harmful-behavior subsets) are normalized into
              one schema, deduplicated, and split into train/val/test under
              dataset/merged_output/. The reproducible_build_config.json and
              quota_audit_report.json beside it document exactly which source
              corpora were sampled, in what proportions, with what random seed.
Why:          A single open corpus would not cover the full attack surface --
              prompt-injection corpora rarely contain jailbreak role-plays,
              jailbreak corpora rarely contain context-poisoning payloads.
              Merging them yields balanced positives across attack types so
              the input gate is evaluated on each class rather than dominated
              by the largest single source.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0.3  adversarial_slice.jsonl           (constructed evaluation slice)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Path:         step4 dataset/adversarial_slice.jsonl
Used by:      run_full_pipeline.py (end-to-end scoring);
              score_slice.py (adaptive-cascade behavioural scoring);
              ablation_grounding.py (Step 10 ablations).
How:          build_adversarial_slice.py samples five labelled groups from
              (0.1) and (0.2) -- a 220-row slice in the default configuration
              (n-adversarial 40, n-poisoned 40, n-benign 80, n-multivector 40,
              n-gate-slip 20) -- and writes them as one shuffled JSONL with
              explicit per-row "expectation" labels:

                kind = "adversarial_query"  : drawn from (0.2) where safety=unsafe.
                                              Tests Threat Model A -- direct
                                              injection. Expectation:
                                              gate_blocks_or_reviews = True.

                kind = "poisoned_context"   : drawn from (0.1) question-answer.
                                              The query itself is benign, but a
                                              poison_chunk string is attached
                                              and the harness splices it into
                                              the retrieved context at Step 5.
                                              Tests Threat Model B -- indirect
                                              injection. Expectation:
                                              poison_redacted_by_step6 = True,
                                              canary_intact_at_step10 = True.

                kind = "benign_control"     : drawn from (0.1) question-answer.
                                              Clean queries with no payload.
                                              Tests SPECIFICITY -- the cascade
                                              must stay at base thresholds.
                                              Expectation:
                                              gate_blocks_or_reviews = False,
                                              sanitization_strictness = 0.50,
                                              rerank_min_score = 0.20.

                kind = "multivector_attack" : a benign question carrying a MILD
                                              query-side instruction PLUS a
                                              SUBTLE poison chunk, each crafted
                                              to score BELOW its own block
                                              threshold so neither vector trips
                                              alone. Tests Threat Model C --
                                              conjunctive sub-threshold
                                              injection. Expectation:
                                              each_vector_subthreshold = True,
                                              multivector_detector_fires = True
                                              (the Section 10A detector must
                                              catch the co-activation).

                kind = "gate_slip_query"    : an unsafe prompt from (0.2)
                                              wrapped in benign-looking framing
                                              (novel-writing, roleplay,
                                              security-training cover). Tests
                                              the SEMANTIC-OBFUSCATION residual:
                                              these carry ~0 injection signature
                                              on both channels, so the detector
                                              is NOT expected to catch them.
                                              Expectation: gate_should_block =
                                              True; the row MEASURES the residual
                                              gap rather than claiming to close
                                              it.
Why:          The rag-mini-wikipedia QA set is almost entirely benign, so
              running it through the full pipeline produces ~0 risk on nearly
              every row, which means Steps 6/7/10 sit at base thresholds and
              the adaptive cascade never fires. To DEMONSTRATE the cascade
              -- the project's defining feature -- you need a mix of inputs
              that actually raise risk in the gate while leaving room for
              benign controls. This slice was constructed to be the smallest
              labelled evaluation set that exercises all of the threat regimes
              simultaneously: the three single-vector regimes above plus the
              conjunctive sub-threshold case (multivector_attack) and the
              semantic-obfuscation residual (gate_slip_query).

Calibration   The injection detector (deberta-v3) is NEAR-BINARY: it scores
finding:      benign text ~0.0 and injection ~0.9-1.0, with little mass in
              between. The [floor, block) band a conjunctive sub-threshold
              attack must occupy is therefore genuinely SPARSE -- in
              calibration runs (calibrate_payloads.py) the in-band poison /
              query candidates frequently come back empty, and the multi-vector
              joint_risk distributions for benign vs attack rows overlap on the
              raw scalar (a single strong context chunk drives joint_risk high
              on its own). This is why the Section 10A detector does NOT key on
              joint_risk alone: it additionally requires at least two channels
              active (min_channels = 2). The near-binary behaviour is reported
              as a finding -- it BOUNDS how often conjunctive sub-threshold
              attacks can be instantiated against this class of detector --
              rather than papered over by lowering thresholds until benign
              rows leak in.


================================================================================
1.  USER INPUT
================================================================================

Purpose. Accept either a live user prompt for serving or, for evaluation,
prompts from the constructed adversarial slice (Section 0.3). Each evaluation
record carries a ground-truth safety label so detector precision and recall
can be measured rather than asserted.

What we use. Python file I/O over the JSONL evaluation slice; FastAPI's
POST /ask endpoint (server.py) for live serving.

Why these tools. JSONL is line-addressable, streamable, and human-readable,
which matters when ground-truth labels need to be re-audited later. FastAPI
was chosen for serving because its Pydantic-based request validation rejects
malformed JSON at the framework boundary (returning a structured 422), so
step 2 never has to defensively re-validate input SHAPE -- it only has to
defend against input CONTENT.  Zero cost.


================================================================================
2.  NORMALIZATION
================================================================================

Purpose. Strip the whitespace, hidden characters, and encoding tricks
attackers use to evade downstream classifiers. A prompt-injection detector
trained on English text cannot reliably score a prompt disguised with
zero-width joiners, bidi overrides, or homoglyphs.

What we use. ftfy (Speer, 2019) to repair mojibake and broken encoding;
Python's unicodedata.normalize("NFKC", ...) for canonical composition and
zero-width / bidi-character stripping; spaCy (Honnibal & Montani, 2017) for
linguistic normalization and contraction expansion. Heuristic Length
Filtering (Butcher et al., 2025) with a 40-2000 character window is applied,
but OUT-OF-WINDOW PROMPTS ARE FLAGGED, NOT DROPPED.

Why these tools. ftfy is the canonical Python library for character-encoding
repair and is deterministic, which means the same input always yields the
same normalized form -- critical for reproducibility of safety experiments.
NFKC was chosen over NFC because it collapses compatibility characters
(fullwidth Latin, ligatures) that attackers use specifically to bypass naive
substring filters. spaCy's tokenizer is mature and ships compact NER models
we reuse downstream in Presidio -- using it here avoids a second tokenization
pipeline.

Point of difference. The Butcher length heuristic was modified: dataset
analysis of (0.2) showed concise inputs (under 40 characters) are FREQUENTLY
genuine attacks ("ignore prior", role-play unlocks) that must still reach
the detector. Hard-dropping them would have created a trivial bypass. The
identical normalization routine runs at training and inference time to
eliminate train/serve skew. All tools open-source, CPU-only, zero cost.


================================================================================
3.  INJECTION DETECTION -- INPUT GATE
================================================================================

Purpose. Every prompt is screened for injection and jailbreak attempts BEFORE
the LLM is ever invoked. A red verdict ends the request immediately and
returns a fixed refusal; the model never sees the prompt at all. This is the
single most important latency win in the pipeline -- most attacks die here,
cheaply.

What we use.
  Step 3   -- the LLM-Guard library's PromptInjection and Toxicity scanners,
              which load the open model protectai/deberta-v3-base-prompt-
              injection-v2 locally.
  Step 3c  -- a local Llama-Guard 3 (1B) classifier via Ollama.
  Fusion   -- a fusion gate (C3RF) that combines them into a single tri-state
              decision (allow / review / block) with category-aware weighting.

Why these tools. protectai/deberta-v3-base-prompt-injection-v2 is the best-
published open injection detector that runs on CPU in single-digit-hundred-
millisecond range -- it replaces a hosted moderation API call with a local
model that is auditable and never sends the prompt off-host. Llama-Guard 3
contributes a CATEGORIZED safety verdict (S1 violent crimes, S2 non-violent
crimes, S5 specialized advice, etc.) rather than a single scalar, which lets
the fusion gate apply category-specific hard blocks rather than relying
solely on a continuous threshold. Combining them rescues both detectors'
failure modes: DeBERTa over-flags benign instruction-following; Llama-Guard
under-flags subtle role-play jailbreaks.

Which dataset trains/tests this stage. The compiled attack dataset (0.2).
Threshold calibration and category-weight tuning are scored against that
JSONL's "safety" and "attack_type" columns.

Point of difference and connection to the pipeline. The gate emits a
CONTINUOUS fused risk score, not merely a binary verdict, and this score is
written into state.scores["fusion_risk"] and propagated forward. A
borderline prompt (risk 0.55) does not get blocked here -- it raises the
strictness of context sanitization (Step 6), reranking (Step 7), and
grounding (Step 10) for the same request. MIT-licensed, local, zero cost.


================================================================================
4.  QUERY EMBEDDING
================================================================================

Purpose. Encode green prompts into dense semantic vectors so the vector
store can find documents that are SEMANTICALLY close to the question, not
merely lexically overlapping. This is what makes RAG retrieve evidence
rather than keywords.

What we use. A local Matryoshka-capable embedding model -- BAAI/bge-m3
(default) via sentence-transformers, with optional Qwen3-Embedding fallback
through Ollama. The vector is truncated to a chosen prefix dimension
(default 512) and L2-renormalized.

Why this tool. bge-m3 is a top-tier open multilingual embedder that supports
Matryoshka representation learning, meaning a single trained model can
produce vectors at ANY prefix length down to 64 dimensions without
retraining. This lets the same model serve a memory-tight HNSW deployment
and a high-recall flat-index deployment from one set of weights. It directly
replaces OpenAI text-embedding-3-large -- an outbound API call that would
otherwise leak every user prompt to a third party. Local, CPU-or-GPU,
zero cost.


================================================================================
5.  VECTOR SEARCH
================================================================================

Purpose. Match the query vector against the indexed knowledge base by
approximate-nearest-neighbour cosine similarity to retrieve candidate
evidence chunks.

What we use. FAISS -- IndexFlatIP over L2-normalized vectors for exact
cosine on smaller corpora; IndexHNSWFlat for true approximate nearest
neighbour on larger corpora. The persisted index lives as kb_wiki.faiss +
kb_wiki.texts and is loaded once at server startup so per-request latency
excludes index load.

Which dataset populates this index. rag-mini-wikipedia / text-corpus from
Section 0.1(a). Every passage is embedded once at --ingest time and never
re-embedded at serve time.

Why this tool. FAISS is the de-facto standard for local vector search --
C++-backed (fast), CPU-and-GPU capable, and gives exact-cosine and ANN
options from one library, so the same code path serves both small-evaluation
and large-deployment regimes. It replaces Pinecone, a paid managed service
that would charge per query and store the corpus off-host. Choosing inner
product on L2-normalized vectors gives mathematically exact cosine
similarity without the cost of a separate normalization step at query time.
Local, zero cost.


================================================================================
6.  RETRIEVED CONTEXT SANITIZATION -- FIRST CHECKPOINT
================================================================================

Purpose. Each retrieved chunk is independently scanned for embedded
instructions before it may enter the prompt. This is the defense against
INDIRECT INJECTION -- attacks smuggled into the knowledge base by a poisoned
document author, the most insidious class of RAG attack because it bypasses
the input gate entirely.

What we use. The same LLM-Guard injection scanner applied PER CHUNK rather
than per query, plus a programmatically inserted CANARY TOKEN appended to
the assembled context.

Why these tools. Reusing the LLM-Guard scanner at this stage rather than
introducing a second detector is deliberate -- running the same model
against the same threat surface keeps the failure modes correlated and
well-understood, and avoids the calibration drift you get from two different
scoring scales. The canary is a unique synthetic token (e.g.
RAGGUARD_CANARY_7f3a...) that has no business appearing anywhere in the
generated answer; if it does, that proves the model treated the context as
instructions to follow.

Which dataset exercises this stage. The poisoned_context rows of (0.3).
Those rows attach a known indirect-injection payload (one of five standard
poison strings) and the per-row expectation poison_redacted_by_step6 = True
makes the test pass/fail observable.

Point of difference and adaptive coupling. The per-chunk scanning
STRICTNESS is set dynamically from the input risk score carried forward
from Step 3c -- if the gate found the question borderline-suspicious, even
mildly suspicious chunks are dropped here. This is a dual-checkpoint
design: the canary planted at Step 6 enables a second, independent
verification at Step 10. Local, zero cost.


================================================================================
7.  CONTEXT RANKING
================================================================================

Purpose. Surviving chunks are reranked for true relevance to the query so
the most pertinent evidence reaches the generator and irrelevant-but-
superficially-similar chunks are demoted. Bi-encoder retrieval (Step 5)
optimizes for recall; this step optimizes for precision.

What we use. A local cross-encoder reranker -- BAAI/bge-reranker-v2-m3 via
sentence-transformers, with Qwen3-Reranker as an alternative.

Why this tool. Cross-encoders read query and chunk JOINTLY (one transformer
pass over the concatenated pair) rather than encoding them separately, which
is significantly more accurate for relevance scoring than dot-product of
bi-encoder embeddings -- at the cost of latency that is acceptable for
top-K reranking but prohibitive for full-corpus retrieval. This is the
standard two-stage retrieval pattern (cheap recall, expensive precision).
bge-reranker-v2-m3 directly replaces Cohere Rerank, a per-query paid API.
Local, zero cost.

Adaptive coupling. The rerank score threshold tightens when
state.input_risk() is high -- under attack we would rather return less
context than risk passing along marginal chunks. score_slice.py asserts
this directly: high-risk rows must show rerank_min_score = 0.40 while
benign rows stay at 0.20.


================================================================================
8.  AUGMENTED PROMPT
================================================================================

Purpose. Assemble the query and the top-ranked evidence into a structured
prompt that EXPLICITLY INSTRUCTS THE GENERATOR NEVER TO OBEY INSTRUCTIONS
FOUND INSIDE THE RETRIEVED CONTEXT. This is the model-side complement to
Step 6: even if a malicious instruction slipped through context
sanitization, the system prompt tells the model to treat retrieved text as
data, not commands.

What we use. LangChain Core's LCEL (|-piped) prompt template.

Why this tool. LCEL templates are declarative, composable, and unit-
testable, and integrate natively with the RunnableParallel we use in Step 9
to fan out to multiple models. Building this layer with raw f-strings would
have made the three-model ensemble in Step 9 significantly more complex to
wire. Open-source, zero cost.


================================================================================
9.  GENERATOR LLM -- PARALLEL INTELLIGENCE
================================================================================

Purpose. Dispatch the augmented prompt concurrently to a SMALL ENSEMBLE OF
INDEPENDENT LOCAL MODELS so the final answer is not at the mercy of one
model's quirks, biases, or jailbreak susceptibility.

What we use. Three Ollama-served open models -- llama3.2:3b, mistral:7b,
qwen2.5:3b -- invoked in parallel through LangChain's RunnableParallel,
with consensus (majority) or fusion selection of the final answer.

Why these tools. Ollama provides a uniform local inference server for
diverse model families, which lets us pick models from three DIFFERENT
training lineages (Meta, Mistral, Alibaba) so their failure modes are
uncorrelated. If a single base model had been jailbroken on a particular
prompt class during training, all three are unlikely to have been
jailbroken in the same way. RunnableParallel runs them concurrently rather
than sequentially so total latency tracks the slowest model, not the sum.
Together they replace GPT-4o (or any hosted model) -- and pointedly, they
let the system run ENTIRELY OFFLINE with no prompts leaving the host.
Zero cost; local compute only.

Point of difference and key signal. The DISAGREEMENT between model outputs
(computed via an all-MiniLM-L6-v2 semantic distance) is itself carried
forward as a risk signal into state.scores["disagreement"]. The principle:
injected or hallucinated content tends to produce INCONSISTENT answers
across architecturally different models, where genuine grounded answers
converge. This disagreement score tightens the grounding judge's threshold
in Step 10.


================================================================================
10. GROUNDING AND POLICY CHECKING -- SECOND CHECKPOINT
================================================================================

Purpose. Verify that the selected answer is faithfully supported by the
retrieved evidence (not hallucinated), and RE-CHECK THE CANARY TOKEN from
Step 6 to confirm no indirect-injection leak occurred during generation.

What we use. A HYBRID FAITHFULNESS SCORER -- a deterministic lexical-overlap
check combined by max with a small-model LLM judge in the DeepEval style --
plus a policy gate.

Why this design. The hybrid was adopted after experiments showed each
scorer alone was unreliable in opposite directions: the small judge
under-scored near-verbatim answers (it preferred paraphrases as evidence
of "understanding"), and the lexical check under-scored legitimate
paraphrases. Crucially, THEIR FAILURES ARE UNCORRELATED -- when one scorer
is fooled, the other tends to be right. Combining them by max rescues both
honest cases while still requiring genuinely ungrounded content to fail
BOTH scorers to slip through. A single hosted judge (e.g. GPT-4 as critic)
would have been simpler but would have re-introduced an outbound API call
and re-coupled the system's safety to a closed model's quirks.

Which dataset exercises this stage. The benign_control and poisoned_context
rows of (0.3), scored against their ground_truth answers (sourced from
0.1 question-answer). ablation_grounding.py runs the lexical-only,
judge-only, and hybrid variants over the same slice to establish that the
hybrid actually outperforms either alone.

Adaptive coupling. The pass threshold is RAISED when input risk or ensemble
disagreement is high -- under attack, we demand stronger evidence of
grounding before releasing the answer. Local judge, zero cost.


================================================================================
10A. MULTI-VECTOR RISK FUSION
================================================================================

Purpose. Catch the CONJUNCTIVE SUB-THRESHOLD attack -- the case a single-
channel gate cannot see. An attacker can split a payload so that the query-
side instruction is too mild to trip the input gate (Step 3c) AND the
context-side poison is too mild to be dropped by sanitization (Step 6), yet
together they steer the answer. Neither channel alone justifies a block; the
co-activation does.

What we use. multivector.py fuses two channels, each in [0, 1]:
    query_vector   = max(injection_detection, fusion_risk)   -- the direct
                     injection / harm signal carried from Steps 3 / 3c.
    context_vector = context_injection  -- the max per-chunk injection score
                     produced by Step 6's sanitizer.
The detector fires only when ALL THREE conditions hold:
    1. each "active" channel is at or above ITS OWN floor (per-channel, set
       from that channel's benign noise -- not one global number);
    2. at least min_channels are active (default 2) -- this is the
       co-activation requirement;
    3. the noisy-OR joint risk (1 - product of (1 - channel)) is itself at or
       above joint_min.
A separate, higher joint_block level (default 0.85) marks the result a HARD
BLOCK rather than merely multi-vector.

Why this design. Condition (2) is what makes a LOW per-channel floor safe.
The injection detector is near-binary (Section 0.3 calibration finding), so a
single strong chunk pushes the noisy-OR joint_risk near 1.0 on its own; keying
on joint_risk alone would therefore false-fire on ordinary poisoned-context
rows. Requiring two channels active separates "one loud vector" (handled by
the existing single-channel rails) from "two quiet vectors acting together"
(the gap this detector exists to close). The four constants
(DEFAULT_SOFT_PER_CHANNEL, DEFAULT_MIN_CHANNELS, DEFAULT_JOINT_MIN,
DEFAULT_JOINT_BLOCK) are the single source of truth and are recalibrated from
calibrate_payloads.py / calibrate_thresholds.py against the benign vs
multivector_attack distributions; the pipeline wiring never changes.

Which dataset exercises this stage. The multivector_attack rows of (0.3),
with benign_control rows as the specificity counter-set. verify_channels.py
confirms both channels are actually populated on accepted multi-vector rows
before any threshold is trusted.

Scope boundary (stated so the writeup does not overclaim). This detector
closes the conjunctive sub-threshold gap ON THE INJECTION CHANNELS only. It
does NOT close pure semantic obfuscation -- the gate_slip_query rows
(roleplay / novel / security-training framing) leave ~0 injection signature
on both channels, so they are reported as a residual gap (Section 0.3),
not as something this stage catches. Local, zero cost.


================================================================================
10B. CLOSED-LOOP RISK-FEEDBACK CONTROLLER
================================================================================

Purpose. Turn the retrieval-to-grounding segment (Steps 4-10) from a single
forward pass into a closed loop that can REVISE the security verdict using the
late signals the forward pass already computes but would otherwise discard.
Prior iterative-RAG work (Self-RAG, Corrective-RAG, FLARE) loops to improve
answer QUALITY; here the control variable is RISK.

What we use. risk_feedback_controller.py wraps the segment. After each attempt
it reads (grounding passed, canary intact, faithfulness, threshold) from Step
10 and the attack-evidence signals (ensemble disagreement from Step 9, the
Section 10A multi-vector verdict, and an opt-in context-coherence anomaly) and
selects exactly one action:

    refuse_multivector       -- Section 10A hard_block fired: coordinated
                                co-activation, refuse now, no retry.
    refuse_security_canary   -- the Step 6 canary leaked into the answer:
                                strongest breach evidence, refuse, no retry.
    accept                   -- grounded with no attack-like late signal.
    escalate_verify          -- grounded BUT attack-like signal: raise
                                effective_risk and RE-RUN STEP 10 ONLY at the
                                now-stricter threshold (no second 3-model
                                generation); accept iff it re-passes.
    refuse_security_signal   -- ungrounded AND attack-like: the gate missed it,
                                the loop catches it, refuse on security grounds.
    recover_retry            -- ungrounded but signals benign and evidence is
                                at least topically related: broaden retrieval
                                (larger k, larger top_n) and retry the full
                                segment, bounded by max_attempts.
    refuse_ungrounded        -- ungrounded with no plausible recovery (out of
                                corpus, or retries / k-cap exhausted): an HONEST
                                refusal, not a security verdict.

Why this design. Escalation does not duplicate the forward cascade's adaptive
logic: the controller simply writes state.scores["effective_risk"], and
PipelineState.input_risk() folds it in, so Steps 6/7/10 re-tighten on the next
pass automatically. The accept/escalate/recover/refuse split is what lets the
loop tell "the gate under-scored an attack" (escalate then refuse) apart from
"benign question, retrieval just missed" (broaden then retry) -- without that
distinction every ungrounded result would be either wrongly refused or wrongly
retried. The recover path is cost-guarded: it refuses immediately rather than
paying for another ensemble generation when faithfulness shows the evidence is
not even topically close (an out-of-corpus question cannot be rescued by a
bigger k).

Cost model. Common path (grounded, clean) = 1 segment run, 1 generation.
Escalate-and-re-verify = 1 segment + 1 Step-10 re-run, no extra ensemble call.
Recover = up to max_attempts segment runs (default 3: 1 initial + 2 retries).

Evaluation hook. run_full_pipeline.py exposes --no-controller, which preserves
the original single-pass behaviour byte-for-byte. Running the same slice with
and without the controller (grounded_baseline.jsonl vs grounded_controller.jsonl)
is the A/B that isolates the controller's effect. The controller is FAIL-CLOSED:
if it raises for any reason, the row is forced to a block rather than served.
Local, zero cost on the common path.


================================================================================
11. OUTPUT SANITIZATION
================================================================================

Purpose. A two-layer output rail that catches PII and unsafe content the
generator may have produced even from clean inputs (training data leakage,
accidental memorization, or rare confabulation).

What we use.
  Layer 1 -- Microsoft Presidio (regex + spaCy NER via en_core_web_lg) to
             mask sensitive PII -- emails, phone numbers, SSNs, credit-card
             fragments, names in sensitive contexts.
  Layer 2 -- a local Llama-Guard classifier for a single-pass safe/unsafe
             verdict on the masked output.

Why these tools. Presidio is the most mature open-source PII scrubber and
is configurable per entity type -- which matters because we DELIBERATELY
EXCLUDE TEMPORAL ENTITIES from masking (dates and times are almost always
legitimate answer content, never PII, and masking them would have destroyed
half the Wikipedia-style answers). Layering Llama-Guard on top catches
semantic unsafety that regex/NER cannot -- slurs in context, harmful
advice, etc. Together they replace hosted moderation APIs (OpenAI
Moderation, Perspective). Local, zero cost.


================================================================================
12. DLP SCANNER
================================================================================

Purpose. A deterministic, model-free data-loss-prevention net positioned
AFTER Presidio specifically so that if the model-based layer ever silently
degrades (Ollama crashes, NER model fails to load), there is still a hard
regex/checksum floor that cannot be bypassed.

What we use. Three layers:
  (a) regex pattern matching for known structured secrets;
  (b) the LUHN CHECKSUM validation to confirm and de-false-positive credit
      card numbers (the regex catches DIGIT SHAPE; Luhn confirms it is a
      REAL card number);
  (c) EXACT-DATA-MATCH against a local sensitive-value store (specific
      known secrets that must never be emitted).

Why this design. Determinism. Steps 3, 6, 9, 10, and 11 all involve ML
models that can fail open if Ollama is down or a model file is corrupted.
Step 12 has no model at all -- it is pure Python and will produce the same
verdict on the same input forever. This is the FAIL-SAFE LAYER OF LAST
RESORT, by design unsexy. Zero cost.


================================================================================
13. SAFE RESPONSE
================================================================================

Purpose. Format a clean answer for the user, and on any block decision at
any prior stage, return the FIXED REFUSAL with the originating stage
recorded for audit.

What we use. A Python formatter and the constant FIXED_REFUSAL.

Why this design. A fixed refusal string is intentional -- it gives an
attacker zero side-channel information about which stage blocked them,
why, or how close they came. Variable error messages are a known
information leak in security systems. The blocking stage is recorded in
the audit trail returned to the operator (block_stage, block_reason) but
NOT in the user-facing answer. Python formatter, zero cost.

ARCHITECTURAL SUMMARY
================================================================================

  Stage                       Tool / Library                       Replaces (paid)            Cost
  --------------------------  -----------------------------------  -------------------------  ----
   1. User Input              FastAPI / JSONL                      --                         $0
   2. Normalization           ftfy, unicodedata, spaCy             --                         $0
   3. Input Gate              LLM-Guard (DeBERTa) + Llama-Guard 3  OpenAI Moderation          $0
   4. Query Embedding         bge-m3 (sentence-transformers)       OpenAI text-embedding-3    $0
   5. Vector Search           FAISS (Flat / HNSW)                  Pinecone                   $0
   6. Context Sanitization    LLM-Guard per-chunk + canary         --                         $0
   7. Reranking               bge-reranker-v2-m3                   Cohere Rerank              $0
   8. Augmented Prompt        LangChain LCEL                       --                         $0
   9. Generator Ensemble      Ollama x {llama3.2, mistral, qwen}   GPT-4o                     $0
  10. Grounding               Lexical-max-DeepEval-judge           GPT-4-as-critic            $0
  10A. Multi-Vector Fusion    multivector.py (noisy-OR co-activn)  --                         $0
  10B. Risk-Feedback Control  risk_feedback_controller.py          --                         $0
  11. Output Rail             Presidio + Llama-Guard               OpenAI Moderation          $0
  12. DLP                     regex + Luhn + EDM                   Symantec / Forcepoint DLP  $0
  13. Safe Response           Python formatter + fixed refusal     --                         $0

The thread that ties every stage together is the carried-forward risk:
state.scores["fusion_risk"] (from Step 3c) and state.scores["disagreement"]
(from Step 9) are read by Steps 6, 7, and 10 to tighten their thresholds
adaptively. A clean prompt costs almost nothing; a borderline one pays for
stricter scrutiny at every downstream stage; an obviously hostile one dies
at Step 3 before the expensive stages are ever invoked. The system is
therefore ADAPTIVE IN STRICTNESS BUT CONSTANT IN SURFACE AREA -- and free
to run.

Two extensions deepen that thread without breaking it. The multi-vector
fusion detector (10A) reads the SAME carried-forward channels -- the query
gate score and Step 6's per-chunk context score -- and fires on their
conjunction, covering the sub-threshold gap a single-channel view misses.
The risk-feedback controller (10B) closes the loop: it writes
state.scores["effective_risk"] from the late signals (disagreement, canary,
grounding, multi-vector verdict), which input_risk() folds back so the same
forward adaptivity re-runs at a stricter setting. Both reuse the existing
risk plumbing rather than introducing a parallel one, so the audit JSONL
still records one risk story per row.
